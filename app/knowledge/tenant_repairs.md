# Tenant repair knowledge base (Netherlands)

Source material for the tenant chatbot: what a tenant can safely solve themselves, when to stop and report, how urgent it is,
and who normally pays. Written in English; the chatbot answers in the tenant's language (Dutch or English). Dutch terms are
given so the chatbot recognises how tenants describe the problem.

Status: draft knowledge, researched 12 Nov 2026. Not legal advice. Items marked **[FILL IN]** need the company's own details.

---

## 0. Rules for the chatbot

1. **Safety first.** If the message mentions gas smell, smoke, fire, sparks, burning smell, carbon monoxide, a tenant feeling
   dizzy/sick with the heating on, water near electricity, or a person in danger: give the emergency steps in section 1 *first*,
   before anything else, and never suggest DIY.
2. **Only safe DIY.** Suggest only steps a non-technical person can do without tools beyond a bucket, cloth, plunger,
   radiator key or screwdriver. Never suggest opening the boiler casing, the meter cupboard behind seals, gas pipes, electrical
   wiring, or working on a roof or ladder above 2 m.
3. **Who pays is a suggestion, not a ruling.** Say "usually" and that the rental contract and the landlord decide. Do not
   promise that the landlord will pay or that the tenant will be charged.
4. **Always offer to report.** If the self-help steps don't work, or the tenant prefers not to try, offer to create a repair
   report and ask for: what, where in the home, since when, photos or a short video, and when someone can get in.
5. **Written report matters.** A repair report in the portal counts as a written report. Remind the tenant to report defects
   promptly; a late report can make the tenant responsible for extra damage.
6. **Don't diagnose fault codes you don't know.** Boiler error codes differ per brand and model. Give only the general steps
   in 3.1 and suggest reporting with a photo of the display.
7. **Keep the tenant's own items out of scope.** The tenant's own appliances (washing machine, fridge, TV, internet router)
   are not the landlord's repair, unless they were rented with the home.

## 3. Heating and hot water

### 3.1 No heating or no hot water (*cv doet het niet, geen warm water, ketel in storing*)
Most boilers in Dutch homes are gas combi-boilers (*cv-ketel, combiketel*). Check in this order:

1. **Thermostat.** Is it set higher than the room temperature? Does the display work? If not, **replace its batteries**.
   Is it on a schedule or holiday mode?
2. **Boiler display.** Is there a fault code or a flashing light? Take a photo of it.
3. **Water pressure.** Look at the pressure gauge (*manometer*) on or below the boiler. Normal is **1.5–2 bar** when the
   heating is cold. **Below 1 bar** the boiler usually stops. If it's low, top it up (3.2).
4. **Restart.** Press the reset button (*resetknop*, often marked "reset" or a flame/arrow symbol) **once**. If there's no
   reset button, switch the boiler off and on with its plug or switch. Wait a few minutes.
5. **Power to the boiler.** Is the plug in? Has a group tripped in the meter cupboard (see 4.1)?
6. **Gas.** Do the gas hobs work? If not, there may be a gas supply problem: report it (or call 0800-9009 if the whole street
   has no gas).
7. **Frost.** In freezing weather, the condensation drain pipe (*condensafvoer*) can freeze, so the boiler stops.
   Don't try to fix this yourself; report it as urgent.

**Stop and report if:** the fault comes back after one reset, there's a fault code, you smell gas, there's water under the
boiler, or the boiler makes banging or whistling noises. Never open the boiler casing.

**Who usually pays:** bleeding radiators, topping up water and restarting after a fault are the tenant's small jobs. Repair,
servicing and replacement of the boiler are for the landlord.

**District heating** (*stadsverwarming, blokverwarming*): there is no boiler in the home. If the heating is off, check the
thermostat and the radiator valves, then report. The fault may be in the whole building.

### 3.2 Topping up the boiler pressure (*cv bijvullen, druk te laag*)
1. Switch the heating off and let it cool down.
2. Find the fill tap (*vulkraan*). On many boilers it's a small tap or blue knob under the boiler. On older systems you need a
   fill hose (*vulslang*) from the cold water tap to the fill valve.
3. Open it slowly and watch the gauge until it shows **1.5–2 bar**. **Use cold water only.**
4. Close the tap. If you used a hose, close both ends first, then remove it over a bucket.
5. Switch the heating back on and reset the boiler if needed.

**Stop and report if:** you have to top up more than about **twice a year**, or the pressure drops within days or weeks. That
points to a leak. Also report if the pressure goes **above 2.5–3 bar** or water drips from the safety valve.

### 3.3 A radiator stays cold, or gurgles (*radiator wordt niet warm, borrelt, ontluchten*)
1. Make sure the radiator valve or thermostat knob is open.
2. **Bleed it:** turn the heating on fully until the radiators are warm, then switch it off and wait 10–15 minutes.
3. Hold a cloth under the bleed valve (*ontluchtingsnippel*, at the top corner) and turn it a quarter turn anticlockwise with
   a radiator key (*radiatorsleutel*, sold at any DIY store).
4. Air hisses out. Close the valve as soon as water comes out.
5. Do the other radiators too, starting at the lowest floor.
6. **Check the boiler pressure afterwards** and top up if needed (3.2).

**Stop and report if:** the radiator is still cold after bleeding while others are warm, or a valve or radiator leaks.
**Who usually pays:** bleeding is the tenant's job; a broken valve or radiator is for the landlord.

### 3.4 Boiler fault codes (*storingscode, foutcode op de ketel*)
Codes differ per brand **and per model**. The chatbot may explain only the codes below, which were checked in the
manufacturers' manuals or in several independent sources. For any other code: ask for a photo of the display and the brand
name on the boiler, give the general steps in 3.1, and report it.

| Brand | Code | Meaning | What the tenant can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vaillant** (ecoTEC) | **F.22** | Too little water / water pressure too low | Top up to 1.5–2 bar (3.2), then reset once. Report if it returns. |
| Vaillant | **F.28** | No ignition at start-up | Check whether the gas hob works (gas supply). Reset once. Report if it returns. |
| Vaillant | **F.29** | Flame goes out during operation | Reset once. Report if it returns. |
| **ATAG** (i-series) | **118** | Water pressure too low | Top up to 1.5–2 bar (3.2), reset once. Report if it returns. |
| ATAG | **133** | No flame after several ignition attempts | Check the gas hob. Reset once. Report if it returns. |
| **Nefit** | **0H, -H, 0A, =H** (and similar with "H") | **Not a fault**: normal operating status (standby, heating, hot water) | Nothing to do; if there's still no heat, see 3.1. |
| Nefit | **6A** | Ignition or flame fault | Check the gas hob. Reset once. Report if it returns. |
| **Remeha, Intergas, others** | any | Varies per model | Remeha and Intergas usually show the water pressure on the display: if it's below 1 bar, top up (3.2). Otherwise photo + report. |

General rule: **reset at most once or twice.** A fault that keeps coming back needs a technician; repeated resets can hide
a gas or flue problem.

### 3.5 Electric water heater, close-in boiler and heat pump (*elektrische boiler, close-in boiler, warmtepomp*)
Not every home has a gas combi-boiler. Ask what the tenant has, or what's written on the appliance.

**Electric water heater** (*elektrische boiler*, a tall cylinder of 50–150 litres, often in a cupboard or attic):
- After a lot of hot water use (several showers, a bath) it needs **a few hours to heat up again**. That's normal.
- Check that its plug is in and its group in the meter cupboard is on (4.1). Some have a temperature knob; check that it
  hasn't been turned down.
- A little water dripping from the **safety valve** (*overstortventiel*, on the cold-water pipe) while it heats up is
  normal. A constant stream is not: report it.
- **Report** if there's no hot water after a night, if it leaks from the tank, or if a group trips when it heats.

**Close-in boiler** (a small 5–15 litre heater under the kitchen sink): check its plug and socket and the group in the meter
cupboard. It only gives a small amount of hot water at a time. Report if it stays cold or leaks.

**Heat pump** (*warmtepomp*, with an outdoor unit and/or an indoor unit with a hot-water tank):
- A heat pump heats **slowly and evenly**: radiators or floor heating feel lukewarm, not hot. That's normal. Don't turn the
  thermostat up and down a lot; keep a steady temperature (for example 19–20 °C) day and night.
- The hot-water tank runs a **weekly hot programme against legionella** and sometimes switches on an electric element when
  a lot of hot water is used. Higher power use then is normal.
- Check the display for a fault code (photo), whether "holiday", "eco" or "boost" mode is on, and whether its group in the
  meter cupboard is on.
- Keep the **outdoor unit free** of leaves, snow and objects. In winter it defrosts now and then, which can produce steam
  and water; that's normal.
- **Never** change installer settings or switch off legionella protection. **Report** fault codes, no heat or hot water
  after checking the above, ice that stays on the outdoor unit, or unusual noises.
- **Hybrid heat pump** (heat pump + gas boiler): if there's no heat, check the boiler too (3.1).
- **District heating** (*stadsverwarming*): see 3.1.

**Who usually pays:** all of these are part of the home's installation: repair and servicing are the landlord's. Normal use
and checking switches and plugs are the tenant's.

## 4. Electricity

### 4.1 Power gone in part or all of the home (*stroom uitgevallen, geen stroom, groep eruit, aardlek, stoppen doorgeslagen*)
Open the meter cupboard (*meterkast*) and look at the switches.

**A residual-current device (*aardlekschakelaar*) tripped** (a wider switch, often with a "T" or "Test" button, that switches
off several groups at once):
1. Unplug all appliances in the rooms without power, or switch off the groups below that switch.
2. Switch the residual-current device back on.
3. Switch the groups on one by one, then plug the appliances back in one by one.
4. When it trips again, the last thing you switched on or plugged in is the likely cause. Leave that one off.
5. After rain, a wet outdoor socket or garden lamp is a common cause.

**One group tripped** (a single narrow switch, *installatieautomaat*): usually too many appliances on one group (for example
a heater and a kettle) or a faulty appliance. Unplug things in that room, switch the group back on, and spread out the use.

**Stop and report if:** it keeps tripping with all appliances unplugged, the switch won't stay on, you smell burning, a
socket or switch is warm or black, or there's water near electrics. Never open the meter cupboard behind a seal, and never
work on sockets or wiring.

**Who usually pays:** faults in the installation are for the landlord. A faulty appliance of the tenant is the tenant's.

### 4.2 Loose or broken socket, switch, lamp or doorbell (*stopcontact los, schakelaar kapot, deurbel*)
- **Loose** socket, switch or doorbell: tightening the cover is a tenant job, but only with that group switched off.
  If the inside is loose or there are bare wires, report it and don't touch it.
- **Light bulbs** in the home are the tenant's. Bulbs in shared halls, stairs and outside (if not part of service charges) are
  usually also listed as small tenant jobs; in apartment buildings the landlord or VvE often arranges these through the
  service charges.
- **Doorbell:** replacing a battery or push button is the tenant's job; the bell transformer or wiring is the landlord's.

### 4.3 Smoke detector beeping (*rookmelder piept*)
- A **short chirp every 30–60 seconds** usually means the battery is low. Replace the battery, or the whole detector if it's a
  sealed 10-year model that is at the end of its life.
- Test it monthly with the test button. Clean it with a vacuum cleaner now and then.
- **Never remove a smoke detector without replacing it.** Smoke detectors on every floor with a living space are required
  in all Dutch homes since 1 July 2022.
- **Who usually pays:** the landlord places the detectors. Checking them and replacing batteries is usually the tenant's
  job; the contract may say otherwise.
- **Carbon monoxide (CO) alarm** beeping continuously: follow the CO emergency in section 1.

## 5. Water, taps, toilet and drains

### 5.1 Dripping tap (*kraan druppelt, lekt*)
- Close the tap firmly but don't force it.
- Descaling the aerator (*perlator*, the tip of the tap) in vinegar overnight often helps with spraying or a weak flow.
- Replacing a washer (*kraanleertje*) or a cartridge is a small tenant job: close the stop valve under the sink first.
- **Stop and report if** the tap body is cracked, the leak is at the pipe or wall, or the stop valve doesn't close.
- **Who usually pays:** washers and small parts are the tenant's; a major repair or new tap is the landlord's.

### 5.2 Blocked sink, shower or bath drain (*afvoer verstopt, gootsteen loopt niet weg*)
1. Remove hair and dirt from the drain cover. In the shower, lift the grid and clean the drain cup.
2. Use a plunger (*ontstopper, plopper*): fill the sink with a little water, cover the overflow hole with a wet cloth, then
   pump firmly 10–15 times.
3. Clean the U-bend (*sifon, zwanenhals*) under the sink: put a bucket underneath, unscrew it by hand, clean it and screw it
   back, hand tight.
4. Hot water with a few spoons of soda (*soda, natriumcarbonaat*) can dissolve grease in a kitchen drain. Don't use boiling
   water on plastic pipes or a toilet.
5. **Avoid aggressive chemical drain cleaners**: they can damage pipes and are dangerous if a plumber has to open the drain.
   Never mix cleaning products.

**Stop and report if:** several drains are blocked at once, water comes up in the shower when the toilet is flushed, there's a
sewer smell together with a blockage, or water comes up from a floor drain. That points to a blockage further in the building
or the sewer.
**Who usually pays:** clearing drains inside the home, up to the connection to the main sewer, is the tenant's job. A
repair of the drain pipes themselves is the landlord's. A blockage caused by wet wipes, fat or nappies is the tenant's cost.

### 5.3 Blocked toilet (*wc verstopt*)
1. Don't flush again if the water is high.
2. Use a toilet plunger (with the flange); pump firmly a number of times.
3. A bucket of warm (not boiling) water poured in from some height can help.
4. **Stop and report if** it doesn't clear, if other drains are also slow, or if it's the **only toilet** (then it's an
   emergency).
5. Only toilet paper goes in the toilet: no wet wipes, kitchen paper, nappies, fat or sanitary products.
**Who usually pays:** as 5.2.

### 5.4 Toilet keeps running, or doesn't flush (*wc blijft doorlopen, spoelt niet door*)
- Lift the lid of the cistern (if it's a free-standing one) and check whether the float is stuck or the chain is tangled.
- For a built-in cistern (*inbouwreservoir*), remove the flush plate. Behind it there's often a small stop valve; close it to
  stop the water and report.
- **Who usually pays:** small parts such as the flush mechanism rubber are usually the tenant's (minor parts); replacing the
  cistern is the landlord's.

### 5.5 Small leak under a sink or behind the washing machine (*klein lek, druppelt onder de gootsteen*)
1. Put a bowl under it and dry the area.
2. Check whether the U-bend nut is loose (tighten by hand) or the washing machine hose is loose or leaking.
3. Close the stop valve under the sink or the washing-machine tap if it keeps dripping.
4. **Report** if it's a pipe, a connection in the wall, or if it keeps leaking. Moisture under the floor or in walls causes
   damage and mould.
**Who usually pays:** the tenant's own washing machine and its hose are the tenant's. Pipes are the landlord's.

### 5.6 No water or low water pressure (*geen water, weinig waterdruk*)
- Is the water main tap (*hoofdkraan*) fully open?
- Is it only one tap? Then descale the aerator (5.1).
- Do the neighbours have water? If not, it's a network fault: look up the **drinking water company for the region** (their
  fault line is on their website) **[FILL IN per city: e.g. Dunea, Evides, Waternet, Vitens, Brabant Water]**.
- In frost, see 5.7.

### 5.7 Frozen pipes (*bevroren leiding, vorst*)
**Prevent:** in frost, don't set the thermostat below about **14 °C**, even when away. Leave doors to cold rooms (such as
the room with the water meter) slightly open. Close and drain the outside tap (*buitenkraan*) in autumn.
**When a pipe is frozen:**
1. Close the water main tap.
2. Check for cracks or leaks. If there is one, report it before thawing.
3. Thaw gently with a hair dryer, warm cloths or a heater, starting from the tap and working towards the frozen part.
   **Never use an open flame.**
4. Open the main tap slowly and watch for leaks.
**Who usually pays:** frost protection of taps is a small tenant job. Damage caused by not protecting against frost can be
charged to the tenant; a pipe that froze because of poor insulation by the building is the landlord's.

## 6. Damp, mould and ventilation

### 6.1 Mould and condensation (*schimmel, vocht, condens, zwarte plekken*)
Mould comes either from **moisture in the air** (cooking, showering, drying laundry, too little ventilation, a cold home) or
from a **building defect** (leak, rising damp, broken ventilation, poor insulation). The cause decides who acts.

Self-help for small spots:
1. Ventilate every day: keep the window grilles (*ventilatieroosters*) open all the time; air the room after showering or
   cooking; leave the bathroom door slightly open after showering and use the extractor fan.
2. Wipe condensation from windows in the morning.
3. Don't dry laundry indoors without ventilation; keep lids on pans and use the cooker hood, letting it run for a while after
   cooking.
4. Heat all rooms a little; a cold room gets damp more easily. Keep furniture a few centimetres from outer walls.
5. Clean small spots with a mould cleaner (*schimmelreiniger*) from a DIY store, wearing gloves, with a window open.
   Never mix cleaning products (for example chlorine and ammonia).

**Report it** if: the mould comes back quickly despite ventilating, covers a large area, is behind wallpaper or furniture on
an outside wall, there's a damp smell, or there's a visible leak, damp wall or broken ventilation. Send photos and say
which room and since when.

**Who usually pays:** cleaning ventilation grilles and preventing condensation by normal use is the tenant's. Leaks,
rising damp, broken ventilation and poor insulation are the landlord's. Serious mould is a defect that the landlord must
repair; in social and mid-market rent the tenant can ask the Huurcommissie for a temporary rent reduction if it isn't fixed
(see 14).

### 6.2 Mechanical ventilation and heat-recovery ventilation (*mechanische ventilatie, MV-box, WTW, ventilatie maakt herrie*)
- **Never switch the ventilation off.** Low (setting 1) when you are home or away, medium (2) with visitors, high (3) while
  showering, cooking or drying laundry.
- Clean the extraction valves (*afzuigventielen*, the round valves in kitchen, bathroom and toilet) a few times a year with
  warm water and soap. **Don't twist them**: their setting is adjusted for the whole home. Put them back in the same place.
- **Heat-recovery unit (WTW):** clean or replace the filters about every 3–6 months as in the unit's manual (unplug it
  first). Who supplies the filters differs per landlord **[FILL IN: tenant buys them / landlord replaces them]**.
- **Report** if the unit is noisy, doesn't run, shows a fault, or there's a draught or condensation inside it.
- **Who usually pays:** cleaning grilles and valves, and replacing filters, are small tenant jobs unless the contract or
  service charges say otherwise. A broken motor or unit is the landlord's.

### 6.3 Cooker hood (*afzuigkap*)
- Clean the grease filter every 1–2 months (dishwasher or hot soapy water). A carbon filter must be replaced, not washed.
- A broken cooker hood is the landlord's if it came with the home.

## 7. Doors, windows, locks and glass

### 7.1 Locked out or lost key (*buitengesloten, sleutel kwijt*)
- Keys are the tenant's responsibility: new keys and a locksmith for a tenant who is locked out are usually the tenant's
  cost. Ask whether a housemate, family or the landlord has a spare key **[FILL IN: does the office keep spare keys, and
  during which hours?]**.
- If the lock must be replaced for safety (key lost with the address on it), report it.

### 7.2 Lock or door sticks (*slot zit vast, deur klemt*)
- Spray graphite or lock spray into the cylinder (no oil in cylinders); oil hinges.
- Tighten loose handles and hinges with a screwdriver.
- **Report** if the door can't be locked (emergency), the lock is broken, or the door or frame is damaged or warped.
- **Who usually pays:** lubricating and tightening are the tenant's. A broken lock or door is usually the landlord's, unless
  it was damaged by the tenant.

### 7.3 Broken window or glass (*ruit kapot, glas kapot, glasschade, barst in het raam*)
1. Remove loose glass pieces carefully with gloves; tape or cover the window temporarily.
2. If it happened during a break-in or by vandalism, report it to the police (for the insurance).
3. Report it with photos.
**Who usually pays:** this depends on the contract. Many landlords insure glass, sometimes through the service charges
(*glasfonds*); otherwise the tenant pays, often covered by their contents insurance with glass cover (*ruitbreuk*).
**[FILL IN: does the company insure glass, and is it in the service charges?]**

### 7.4 Draught (*tocht*)
- Draught strips (*tochtstrips*) on doors and windows are a small tenant job.
- A window that doesn't close, or a gap in the frame, should be reported.

### 7.5 Condensation or mist *between* the panes of double glazing (*condens tussen het dubbel glas, beslagen ruit binnenin*)
- Condensation on the **inside surface** of a window in the morning is normal: ventilate and wipe it (6.1).
- Mist, droplets or dirt **between** the two panes, which you can't wipe away from either side, means the seal of the double
  glazing has failed. It doesn't insulate well any more.
- **Report it** with a photo. It's not urgent. **Who usually pays:** the landlord (wear), unless the glass was damaged.

### 7.6 Windows and sun blinds (*raam gaat niet open/dicht, hor, zonnescherm, rolluik*)
- Clean and lubricate window hinges and tracks a few times a year.
- Fly screens (*horren*), curtains and blinds put up by the tenant are the tenant's.
- Sun blinds or roller shutters that came with the home: report a broken motor, cord or slat. Don't force them.

## 8. Outside, roof and shared areas

### 8.1 Roof leak or water through the ceiling in rain (*daklekkage*)
- Catch the water, move items away, take photos and report it as urgent (or an emergency if water reaches electricity).
- **Never go onto the roof.**
- **Who usually pays:** the landlord.

### 8.2 Gutters (*dakgoot, regenpijp*)
- Cleaning gutters and downpipes that are safe to reach is listed as a small tenant job; on houses with more than one
  floor the landlord often arranges it. **Never use a ladder on your own above about 2 m.**
- A leaking or broken gutter is the landlord's.

### 8.3 Garden (*tuin*)
- Mowing, weeding, cutting hedges and keeping paths and fences in order are the tenant's jobs.
- Large trees and the fence's construction are usually the landlord's, depending on the contract.

### 8.4 Shared areas in an apartment building (*trappenhuis, lift, portiek, verlichting gang*)
- Broken lift, stairwell lights, front door or intercom: report it. In buildings with a VvE (owners' association) the
  landlord passes it on to the VvE manager.
- Keeping shared areas clean is often arranged through the service charges.

### 8.5 Sewer smell (*rioollucht*)
- A drain that isn't used often dries out: pour a bucket of water in each drain (shower, floor drain, washing machine drain).
- Clean the U-bend (5.2).
- **Report** if the smell remains, especially together with slow drains.

### 8.6 Intercom, door opener, mailbox, storage, bike shed, balcony (*intercom, brievenbus, berging, fietsenstalling, balkon*)
- **Intercom or door opener** (*intercom, deuropener werkt niet*): check whether neighbours have the same problem (then it's
  the building's system) and whether the handset in the home has power or batteries. Report it; it's the landlord's or the
  VvE's.
- **Mailbox** (*brievenbus*): the lock and key of an individual mailbox are small tenant parts; a broken mailbox unit in the
  hall is the landlord's or the VvE's.
- **Storage room or bike shed** (*berging, fietsenstalling*): keep it clean and don't store petrol, gas bottles or chemicals.
  Report a broken door, lock or lighting, leaks or damp.
- **Balcony** (*balkon*): keep the drain hole clear of leaves, don't drill into the floor or railing, and don't put heavy
  tubs or barbecues with open fire against the façade if the house rules forbid it. Report cracks, rust on the railing or
  water that stays on the balcony.

## 9. Pests (*ongedierte, muizen, ratten, kakkerlakken, bedwantsen, wespen*)

| Pest | Self-help | Report when | Who usually pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mice and rats** (*muizen, ratten*) | Store food in closed containers, remove crumbs, close small holes around pipes with steel wool, use traps. | Rats, mice that keep coming back, or visible holes or gaps in the building. | Small-scale control is the tenant's; if the cause is the building (holes, broken sewer), the landlord's. |
| **Cockroaches, bedbugs** (*kakkerlakken, bedwantsen*) | Clean, vacuum, wash bedding hot; don't bring in second-hand furniture without checking. | Always report: these need a professional and may affect neighbours. | Depends on the cause; often decided per case. Report early. |
| **Wasps' nest** (*wespennest*) | Keep away, keep windows closed near it. | Nest in or on the home. | Usually the tenant's when in their part of the home; the municipality sometimes helps. |
| **Pigeons on balcony** | Keep the balcony clean; don't feed birds. | Nests in the roof or ventilation. | Case by case. |
| **Silverfish, moths** | Ventilate, keep dry, vacuum. | Many in a damp room (may be a moisture problem, see 6.1). | Tenant's, unless caused by damp in the building. |

Rule from the decree on small repairs: pest control is the tenant's when the cost is limited **and** the pests are not caused
by the building. The landlord should decide on anything larger.

## 10. Appliances (*apparaten, oven, kookplaat, inductie, vaatwasser, wasmachine, koelkast*)

First ask: **did the appliance come with the home** (listed in the contract or the move-in report), or is it the tenant's own?
- **The tenant's own appliances** (washing machine, dryer, fridge, TV, microwave, router): the tenant's own repair. The
  landlord only handles the connection points (water tap, drain, socket).
- **Appliances that came with the home** (oven, hob, dishwasher, fridge in a furnished home, extractor hood): repair and
  replacement are usually the landlord's, unless the damage was caused by misuse.

| Problem | Self-help | Report when |
|---|---|---|
| **Oven or hob doesn't work** (*oven/kookplaat doet het niet*) | Check the group in the meter cupboard and the oven's timer or child lock (often a key symbol: hold it for a few seconds). | It stays dead, a group trips, a burner doesn't ignite, or there's a gas smell (section 1). |
| **Induction hob** (*inductie*) shows an error or switches off | Use only pans with an iron base (a magnet sticks to the bottom). Remove the pan, switch the hob off and on. Keep the vents clear. | Errors stay, or the glass is cracked (stop using it). |
| **Dishwasher** (*vaatwasser*) doesn't drain or clean | Clean the filter at the bottom, check the drain hose isn't kinked, use salt and rinse aid. | It leaks, stays full of water, or doesn't start. |
| **Washing machine** doesn't drain | Clean the pump filter (small flap at the front bottom, with a towel and bowl ready). Check the drain hose. | Water leaks from the tap or wall drain (that part is the landlord's). |
| **Fridge or freezer** | Check the plug and temperature setting; defrost a freezer with a lot of ice; keep the back clear. | It stays warm (if it came with the home). |

## 11. Bathroom and kitchen wear (*kitnaad, tegel, douchekop, wc-bril, keukenkastje, vloer*)

| Problem (Dutch) | Self-help | Who usually pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Mouldy or loose sealant** (*kitnaad schimmelt, laat los*) around shower or bath | Clean it with a mould cleaner and ventilate (6.1). Replacing a short strip of sealant is a small job. | Cleaning: tenant. Sealant that has come loose by age, or water getting behind tiles: report it; landlord. |
| **Cracked or loose tile** (*tegel kapot, los*) | Don't let water get behind it: keep the area dry. | Report it. Landlord, unless caused by the tenant (dropped object, drilling). |
| **Shower head or hose** (*douchekop, doucheslang*) | Descale in vinegar; replacing them is a small tenant job. | Tenant. |
| **Toilet seat** (*wc-bril*) loose or broken | Tighten the bolts underneath; replacing the seat is a small job. | Tenant. |
| **Bathroom extractor fan** (*afzuiging badkamer*) | Clean the grille. Check its switch or timer. | Broken fan: landlord. |
| **Kitchen cabinet hinge or drawer** (*keukenkastje, scharnier*) | Tighten or adjust the hinge screws. | Small adjustment: tenant. Broken cabinet or worktop by age: landlord. |
| **Silicone around the kitchen sink or worktop** | Keep it dry and clean. | As sealant above. |
| **Floor damage** (*vloer beschadigd*) | Use felt pads, don't drag furniture. | Tenant's own flooring: tenant. Floor of the home: landlord, unless the tenant caused the damage. |

## 12. Questions that are not repairs

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Internet, TV or phone not working** | That's the tenant's own provider (for example KPN, Ziggo, Odido). Contact them. The landlord only handles the cable or fibre connection point in the wall if it's damaged. |
| **Meter readings, energy bill, switching energy supplier** | That's between the tenant and their energy supplier. The fault line 0800-9009 is only for outages and gas smells. If utilities are included in the rent, ask the landlord. |
| **Noisy neighbours** (*geluidsoverlast buren*) | Not a repair. Talk to the neighbours first; if that doesn't help, a neighbourhood mediation service (*buurtbemiddeling*) can help. Report it to the landlord if the neighbours are also tenants of the company. |
| **Waste bins and collection** (*afvalcontainer, ophaaldag*) | The municipality handles bins, collection days and bulky waste (*grofvuil*). See the municipality's website or waste app. |
| **Extra key for a new housemate** | Ask the landlord first: a new housemate may need to be registered. A key copy is at the tenant's cost; some locks need a security card. |
| **Changes to the home** (drilling, painting in a strong colour, new floor, satellite dish) | Small things like hanging pictures are fine. Larger changes need the landlord's permission first, and may have to be undone when moving out. |
| **Moving out** | Give notice in writing as the contract says. The landlord plans a pre-inspection (*voorinspectie*) and a final inspection; damage beyond normal wear is charged. |
| **Rent, invoices, payments** | Use the Payments section of the portal or contact the office. |

## 13. Rented rooms and shared homes (*kamerverhuur, gedeelde keuken/badkamer*)

- **Each tenant** looks after their own room as in this knowledge base (small repairs, cleaning, ventilation).
- **Shared spaces** (kitchen, bathroom, toilet, hall): the housemates keep them clean together. Agree on a cleaning schedule.
  A cleaning issue between housemates is not a repair; the landlord can help if it affects hygiene or safety.
  **[FILL IN: does the company arrange cleaning of shared areas through service charges?]**
- **Shared appliances** that came with the room (fridge, cooker, washing machine in the house): report a breakdown; the
  landlord repairs it, unless someone caused the damage.
- **Blocked shared drain or toilet:** follow 5.2 and 5.3; if nobody did it on purpose, the housemates share the job.
- **Fire safety:** don't block escape routes or fire doors in the hall; don't remove smoke detectors; no extension leads
  chained behind each other.
- **Report safety issues** (smoke detector missing, fire door doesn't close, gas or electricity problems) immediately; they
  affect everyone in the house.

## 14. The tenant's rights and duties (summary for the chatbot)

- **Small repairs are for the tenant.** Dutch law (article 7:217 Civil Code, with the *Besluit kleine herstellingen*) lists
  small, everyday repairs that the tenant does and pays for. Everything else, including defects, major repairs and wear
  from age, is for the landlord (article 7:206). The rental contract can give the landlord more tasks, but not give the
  tenant more than the law allows.

  The decree's list, in short: painting inside, wallpapering and filling small holes; tightening loose parts (handles,
  switches, sockets, doorbell); replacing small parts at limited cost (washers, handles, keys, grilles, shower head and hose,
  toilet seat); oiling and descaling hinges, locks and taps; frost protection of taps; lamps; small glass and mirrors at
  limited cost; bleeding radiators, topping up the heating, restarting after a fault and replacing ventilation filters;
  draught strips; small outside parts (letterbox, outdoor lamp); garden upkeep; sweeping reachable chimneys and ducts;
  clearing internal drains up to the main sewer; cleaning the home and shared areas, windows and frames; pest control at
  limited cost not caused by the building; cleaning reachable gutters; removing litter and graffiti; emptying a septic tank.

- **Damage caused by the tenant** (or by their visitors or pets) is the tenant's cost, even if the repair itself is a landlord
  job. Contents insurance (*inboedelverzekering*) and liability insurance (*aansprakelijkheidsverzekering*) often cover this.
- **Report defects promptly.** The tenant must tell the landlord about a defect. If they don't, they can be liable for extra
  damage.
- **If the landlord doesn't repair:** the tenant should report in writing and give a reasonable deadline. For urgent matters
  that is short; otherwise six weeks is a common starting point. If the landlord then still doesn't repair, the tenant may
  have it repaired and deduct reasonable costs from the rent (article 7:206 paragraph 3), or ask for a rent reduction.
  Recommend the tenant to get advice first (for example from Het Juridisch Loket or the Woonbond) before deducting anything.
- **Huurcommissie** (rent tribunal): for social and mid-market rent, after a written report and six weeks without a repair,
  the tenant can ask for a temporary rent reduction for serious defects. Free-sector tenants go to the court (kantonrechter).
- **Since 1 July 2023** (*Wet goed verhuurderschap*) landlords must give tenants written information, including how to report
  defects and which small repairs the tenant does. This knowledge base and the portal's repair report support that.

## 15. What to ask when creating a repair report

1. **What** is wrong, in the tenant's words, and **where** in the home (room, floor).
2. **Since when**, and whether it's getting worse.
3. **Photos or a short video**, including a photo of any display or fault code.
4. What the tenant **already tried** (from this knowledge base).
5. **Safety:** is there water near electricity, a gas smell, or a person at risk?
6. **Access:** when is someone home, are there pets, may a contractor enter with a key?
7. **Phone number** for the contractor to call.

Then suggest the urgency from section 2 and confirm that the report has been sent.
